Intro

Within the realm of fiction, whether it be books or video games or movies/TV shows, at some point, you are bound to run into a Mary Sue character. Simply put, a Mary Sue is a female character that little to no flaws, who does exceptionally well at whatever the story needs her to do, recieves little to no character development, and is generally a nothing burger character. She is usually the protagonist of the story, and male characters who also meet these criteria are usually referred to as Marty or Gary Stus. For the sake fo some of the points I will be making, however, I will only focus on the Mary Sues.

Now, to be clear, the title isn't SUPER accurate to my feelings on Mary Sues. I do believe they are one of the worst writing tropes, but there are also several worse ones. Still, Mary Sues, at the very least, make the top 10 list for me in that regard, so I'd like to take a deeper dive into this particular type of character.

Why Do They Exist?

The world of Mary Sues is a weird one. Generally speaking, though, their existence can be grouped into three main reasons.

The Author's Self-Insert

One reason for the main character of a story to be a Mary Sue is that they are a self-insert character for the author. Instead of creating an original character with their own thoughts and motivations, the author projects a version of themself that they idealize into the character. Sometimes, this version of a Mary Sue is fairly easy to identify because their design shows obvious similarities with the appearance of the person who wrote them. On top of that, authors of these characters will sometimes get very defensive when face with criticism about how the character is written.

The Reader's Self-Insert

A similar reason that a female protagonist is written as a Mary Sue is that they are designed to be a self-insert for the reader. When this happens, it means that the author purposefully left the character as a mostly blank slate so that the reader could project themselves onto the character instead. It can be argued that this leads to the most bland form of Mary Sue, since they are intentionally given nothing unique to themselves, instead of being a reflection of the writer's (usually heavily flawed) idealised self which is usually percieved as more annoying.

Just Poor Writing

First and foremost, it can very much be argued that the first two reasons are poor writing (I certainly would). But they're intentional, and general poor writing isn't always intentional. In the case of Mary Sues, it's very easy for someone to unintentionally write one. Not everyone thinks to think of their character's motivations and personality. Some authors do but struggle at it. Others may simply be taking inspiration from other Mary Sues without even realizing it. So "poor writing" is functionally just any other unintentional reason.

Why I (and many others) Don't Like Them

Boring To Read/Watch

One of the most widely-shared complaints about Mary Sues is that they're simply boring. Because of their lack of character development, independence, and tend to get pushed around by the plot instead of pushing the plot foreward on their own, there just isn't anything going for them. They're just not entertaining, leading to side characters in the story taking the spotlight in the reader's mind.

Annoying To Read/Watch

Another issue with Mary Sues is that they can be annoying. What will happen a lot of time with Mary Sues is that they'll be described as painfully average, with average looks, average intelligence, average clothes, etc, but then excel at the things that they just described. They'll have average looks and then pull at least two male characters. They'll have average intelligence and then ace all of their classes. In a similar vein, they'll be described as super petite and small and skinny but then be really strong in a fight. And then, of course, there's the author self-insert variant that is especially idealised so that they still have the authors flaws, but then both themself and the story treat them as though they do no wrong.

Sick and Tired of Poorly-Written Women

Poorly-written women, unfortuneately, are nothing new. But that doesn't change that people, iuncluding myself, are sick of it. Some authors think that there is a fundamental differance in writing a character that is a woman versus a man when, for the most part, unless their gender is relevant to their character arc, the storyline, or the worldbuilding, then no! And these Mary Sues often lack agency, and it does say somethingabout how the author sees women. Mary Sues perpetuate the idea that women must be perfect and good at everything, but because they are women, they are not allowed to acknowlege that they are good at thing sor have confidence in themselves. They also add to the idea that women must be able to do things while still fitting into a very strict beauty standard (the earlier example with the skinny petite body but is still somehow the best fighter). It is simply tiring.

It is worth noting that an FMC being a Mary Sue doesn't necesarily make or break a story, necesarily. But it often does.
